# Thetis must prove a payable workflow

**Verdict: narrow the commercial experiment, then proceed conditionally with a paid evidence-and-release pilot.** Thetis has a plausible workflow opportunity inside existing chrome finance, but no demonstrated financing gap that its records uniquely solve. Start with one repeat exporter, an independent custodian or collateral manager, and an existing professional lender. Test exporter-paid service fees against a manager/lender enterprise licence without declaring either the winning buyer. Existing inventory finance, offtake funding and digital collateral products constrain novelty; they do not prove universal access to affordable credit. Physical control, enforceable rights and full customer costs matter more than the receipt format. The open lending pool remains an unvalidated business hypothesis, while the prototype can test partial sale before dispatch and custody of the financed remainder.

**Confidence:** high that category-level novelty and universal “no finance in transit” claims are untenable; moderate that the proposed network is a useful experiment; low that a buyer will pay, evidence is the binding credit constraint, or the lending economics work. These are qualitative judgments, not probabilities.

**Research cutoff: 17 November 2026.** This combines the original Claude report and independent Codex report, with supporting notes and the original project design. It is a synthesis, not a third independent investigation. Agreement based on the same source is not corroboration. No interviews, outreach, quotes, partnerships, customer commitments or measured savings were obtained. Source retrieval/access descriptions are inherited from the research, not fresh verification. Recommendations below do not modify the project or impose a build-phase cutoff. Provenance and disagreements are recorded in the [reconciliation](../research_notes/Thetis%20combined%20business%20validation/reconciliation.md).

## A transaction network comes before a winning payer

### A–B. The opportunity depends on contracts and who carries the stock

The strongest segment hypothesis is a repeat independent chrome trader/exporter carrying lawful, uncommitted inventory with clean title and an existing lender relationship. Its finance lead could buy faster dossier preparation or release coordination. Equally, a collateral manager could buy lower monitoring cost across its engagements, or a lender could buy cheaper borrowing-base administration. These are competing routes to the same transaction network. Test each budget owner and procurement path; do not count the exporter’s passed-through fee and the manager’s licence as separate value created by one avoided task.

Chrome’s commercial structure matters. Large integrated producers, independent producers, UG2 by-product suppliers and juniors do not share one funding problem. Tharisa’s audited FY2024 accounts describe 30–90-day chrome export terms, letters of credit and inventory-secured pre/post-shipment finance. This establishes deferred cash receipt and financing at a named large producer, not SME access. Claude also found historical junior-producer offtake/prepayment examples; the relevant lesson is to inspect existing output commitments before assuming ore is available for a new pledge. Traxys explicitly offers finance alongside trading services. ([Tharisa FY2024](https://www.tharisa.com/pdf/investors/annual-results/2024/ifrs-full-version-with-signed-audit-report-2024.pdf); [Traxys services](https://www.traxys.com/services))

**Added from the Claude original (confirmed 18 November 2026 from the company's announcement):** on 24 March 2026, Tharisa said its trading subsidiary Arxo Resources "has negotiated improved unsecured, revolving trade finance facilities": US$30m from HSBC and US$15m from Absa, "with an accordion of US$15m". They "provide for both pre- and post-shipment finance" and "replace existing, more onerous and traditional trade finance facilities". US$45m is the committed total; the accordion is additional and uncommitted. An established producer can therefore move from secured to unsecured trade finance, which makes such producers weak first customers. The same announcement calls secured trade finance "more onerous", which supports, but does not prove, a friction cost for traders without unsecured lines. ([Tharisa RNS](https://www.ticker.app/lse/THS/rns/2026-03-24/Trade-finance-facilities-with-HSBC-and-Absa-Bank/7447X); [SENS listing](https://www.sharedata.co.za/sens.asp?id=542786); [IOL Business Report, 24 March 2026](https://iol.co.za/business-report/2026-03-24-tharisa-secures-45m-in-new-trade-finance-to-boost-chrome-trading-operations/))

Offtake is a serious substitute, not merely an expensive loan. It can bundle market access, logistics and advance payment while limiting future selling freedom. A named 2026 HMS/Langpan release reports exclusive chrome offtake; the source record does not establish every associated security term. Neither exclusivity alone nor an advance proves that every tonne is legally unavailable for financing. Obtain the agreement, prior-lender consent and collateral schedule. Large producers with bank access are useful process benchmarks; already-prepaid miners and output fully committed to existing financing are weaker first-customer candidates. ([HMS issuer announcement](https://www.webdisclosure.com/press-release/hms-bergbau-ag-etr-hmu-hms-bergbau-signs-exclusive-offtake-agreement-for-chrome-ore-with-south-african-mining-company-MqLDBu2YyF6))

**Incoterms do not determine payment or title.** FCA does not establish that a miner receives cash at collection; that requires the sale contract. Reconstruct four separate timelines: ownership, possession, risk of loss and cash. A transport delay matters financially only to the party funding the exposed asset or receivable. FNB markets inventory, receivables and in-transit finance, and CA-CIB describes financing across sourcing, transportation and distribution. Therefore, ask why a specific eligible applicant cannot obtain acceptable terms; do not infer that everyone creditworthy already can. ([ICC explanation](https://library.iccwbo.org/clp/clp-incoterms-qa-2020.htm?AGENT=ICC_HQ); [FNB](https://www.online.rmb.co.za/business-banking/forex/trade-finance.html); [CA-CIB](https://www.ca-cib.com/en/expertise/solutions-support-your-financing-strategy/facilitating-your-commercial-transactions-2))

**Added from the Claude original: where existing finance is limited.** Three sourced points describe the gap Thetis would test. None proves that a chrome trader is underserved.

- **In-transit stock is a small share.** Lenders hold in-transit inventory in a borrowing-base "basket" that "is typically kept small", because of diversion, carrier liens and jurisdiction risk. This is general practice, not SA-specific. ([Mayer Brown, September 2023](https://www.mayerbrown.com/de/insights/publications/2023/09/mbitesize-goods-in-transit), read in full)
- **Partial release is manual.** Under a collateral management agreement, goods are released when the lender instructs or the debt is repaid: a manual instruction, which is the step Thetis would automate. ([GTR, Understanding collateral management](https://www.gtreview.com/magazine/volume-16issue-2/understanding-collateral-management); [FimBank warehouse finance](https://www.fimbank.com/en/warehouse-finance))
- **Banks grew warier of smaller traders.** After the 2020 Singapore trader collapses, commentators expected banks to be "particularly risk averse with smaller or localised traders". This is commentary, not lending data. ([Daily Maverick/Bloomberg, 9 July 2020](https://www.dailymaverick.co.za/article/2020-07-09-after-9-billion-credit-hit-banks-seek-trade-finance-overhaul/))

The initial network should use one corridor and one existing relationship chain. Exporter treasury owns its cash problem; collateral-manager operations owns field adoption; lender credit/risk accepts the evidence; the terminal controls actual release within its contractual authority. Hauliers need a faster gate, not another unpaid administrative duty. Start with existing records and ordinary mobile interaction, then measure whether co-signing, extraction and dossier assembly reduce work. Named organisations below are discovery routes, not prospects that have expressed interest.

## Existing products leave an integration hypothesis, not an empty category

### D. Compete against finance, control and ordinary software together

World Bank material describes commodity collateral structures and bank-authorised release. Digital entitlement, pledged inventory and debt-first payment are precedents. Thetis’s possible differentiation is their integration into a usable mineral-lot workflow, not invention of the underlying finance. No complete vendor capability audit establishes uniqueness or absence of inland coverage. ([World Bank commodity-backed finance](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/619781498850171182/pdf/Using-commodities-as-collateral-for-finance-commodity-backed-finance.pdf))

| Alternative | What overlaps; evidence/status | Consequence for Thetis |
|---|---|---|
| Bank inventory/transit finance and trader offtake | FNB, CA-CIB and Traxys advertise established finance/service structures | Compare eligibility, net selling price, fees, exclusivity and releases |
| SGS; Steinweg collateral management | Physical monitoring/control services currently marketed | Potential channel and incumbent; software cannot assume away their costs ([SGS](https://www.sgs.com/en/services/periodic-and-long-term-storage-management); [Steinweg](https://www.steinweg.com/services/collateral-management-2/)) |
| MineHub/Surecomp | Commercial integrated post-trade/trade-finance launch announced **4 September 2025**, available to joint customers and being piloted | Closest workflow comparator; exact release capabilities still unverified ([announcement](https://minehub.com/minehub-and-surecomp-launch-integrated-post-trade-and-trade-finance-solutions-bridging-global-commodity-trade-financing/)) |
| Komgo/Mercuria | April 2022 borrowing-base deployment included digital drawdowns, collateral processing and mark-to-market | Not merely an LC/document product; historical deployment does not establish current facility activity ([Komgo](https://www.komgo.io/newsroom/komgo-named-digital-agent-on-first-borrowing-base-in-asia)) |
| TradeCloud | Minerals/metals contracting, communications and workflow offering | Adjacent installed workflow; partial pledged release not established ([TradeCloud](https://tradecloud.sg/)) |
| DMCC Tradeflow; LMEsword | Pledgeable warrants/approved facilities and electronic warrant infrastructure | Strong receipt precedent, bounded by commodity and legal framework ([DMCC](https://dmcc.ae/ecosystems/financial-services/tradeflow); [LME](https://www.lme.com/trading/systems/lmesword)) |
| PAX Gold; Santander/Agrotoken | Allocated-bullion token terms; official 2022 agricultural token-loan pilot | Fractional tokens and commodity-secured lending precedents, not evidence of chrome deployment ([Paxos](https://www.paxos.com/terms-and-conditions/pax-gold-terms-conditions); [Santander](https://www.santander.com/en/press-room/press-releases/2022/03/santander-and-agrotoken-join-forces-to-offer-loans-secured-by-cryptoassets)) |
| Signed-document portal, database and bank escrow | Ordinary database transactions support atomic internal updates | Required counterfactual on cost, trust, recovery and integrations ([PostgreSQL](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/tutorial-transactions.html)) |

The original design permits **partial pledged sale before dispatch**, then movement of the financed remainder. It blocks sale/split during transit or a prepared shipment; marketing “trade pieces while trucks move” would misdescribe it. In the 5,000-tonne example, a 2,000-tonne sale at an assumed USD200/t generates USD400,000, which goes to debt before seller proceeds. Faster paydown is not automatically fresh spending cash. Debt-first releases can still help throughput and financing capacity, but that benefit needs measurement.

Solana can atomically update on-chain receipt, debt and USDC state. Its commercial argument is shared execution and inspectability across institutions, not unique access to transactional consistency. Bank transfers, court orders and ore release remain outside that atomic boundary. Wallet recovery, private evidence storage, conversion, permissions and failed-transaction handling are operating costs. A fiat-compatible workflow is a valid comparison; choosing it commercially would be a recommendation, not a rewrite of the prototype. ([Solana transactions](https://solana.com/docs/core/transactions))

**Added from the Claude original: shared-ledger networks have failed on adoption.** we.trade said its growth was "not quick enough to ensure the network effect needed". Marco Polo's modules were "limited to a handful of transactions" before it went into liquidation in February 2023. Consortia also recognised that their goals "did not require blockchain technology at all". The main risk for a multi-party network is cold-start adoption, not technology. Starting inside one collateral manager's existing engagements avoids that, but it also strengthens the permissioned-database alternative. The chain argument then rests on verifiable state that no single operator controls, and on later interoperability between managers and lenders. ([GTR, lessons learned](https://www.gtreview.com/news/global/lessons-learned-from-industry-on-the-bumpy-road-to-trade-digitalisation/); [GTR on Marco Polo](https://www.gtreview.com/news/fintech/marco-polo-brings-in-liquidators-as-funds-run-dry/))

## Full costs and loss conventions change the result

### E. Customer, software and lender economics must stay separate

All numbers in this section are **hypothetical sensitivities, not market quotes or an empirical base forecast**. The executable model uses 5,000 tonnes at USD200/t, USD1m collateral, a 60% advance and a **USD600,000, 45-day loan**, with actual/365 simple interest. USD200 is an assumed inland value, not a scraped CIF price. Public producer averages and index catalogues cannot substitute for executable grade/location netbacks, current buyer bids or a redistribution licence. ([Tharisa FY2026 production report](https://www.tharisa.com/download/sens-rns-tharisa-q4-fy2026-production-report-13oct26.pdf?1793721278=); [Fastmarkets catalogue](https://help.fastmarkets.com/en_US/metals-price-list))

At an assumed old rate of 24% and new rate of 16%, interest falls from USD17,753.42 to USD11,835.62: **USD5,917.81 saved**. Add assumed labour savings of USD750. The proposed fee is USD500 plus USD0.20/t, or USD1,500. Inspection USD1,500, custody USD1,000, insurance USD500, conversion/on-off-ramp USD3,000, legal/onboarding USD1,000 and adoption labour USD500 bring total assumed added costs to **USD9,000**. Net customer benefit is consequently **minus USD2,332.19**. None of these fee levels or savings has been measured.

This deliberately tests a costly case; it does not justify charging existing costs twice. Compare invoices and tasks under both workflows. Inspection already required by the lender is baseline expenditure unless Thetis adds frequency or scope. The same applies to insurance and custody. Removing only USD3,000 conversion cost changes benefit to **plus USD667.81**; treating USD3,000 inspection/custody/insurance as already incurred has the same arithmetic effect. Removing both groups gives plus USD3,667.81. These are separate counterfactuals, not verified savings. At the original full incremental cost, break-even requires a **11.15 percentage-point annual rate reduction**, after the assumed labour saving. With an eight-point reduction, net benefit ranges from minus USD4,304.79 at 30 days to plus USD3,585.62 at 90 days. Longer duration also consumes more capital and exposure time.

**Added in review: the incumbent-rate sensitivity.** The independent Codex report also tested a 12% incumbent rate (net benefit minus USD11,208.90). With other inputs unchanged and a 16% new rate, a 15% incumbent gives minus USD8,989.73 and a 10% incumbent gives minus USD12,688.36. An exporter with an existing bank line is more likely to pay near these lower rates. Claude's sources put SOFR at about 3.9% and SA prime at 10.75%, and a credit-insured chrome-trader loan priced about 102 bp all-in over SOFR ([TXF, July 2026](https://www.txfnews.com/news/74668/Pricing-emerges-on-Gerald-Groups-ECA-backed-loan)). No source shows that better custody evidence lowers the interest rate, so the eight-point reduction is an assumption. The 90-day case also holds custody and insurance fixed although both normally accrue with time.

The lender models in the source reports use **different loss conventions**. Claude assumes an annual expected-loss rate; Codex assumes per-loan probability of default multiplied by loss given default. Comparing their headline profits without normalising principal, tenor, rates and servicing is misleading. Holding all those common inputs fixed gives:

| Common USD600,000 / 45-day loan; borrower 16%, capital 10%, servicing USD1,000 | Annual expected-loss convention | Per-loan PD × LGD convention |
|---|---:|---:|
| Interest received | 11,835.62 | 11,835.62 |
| Capital cost | 7,397.26 | 7,397.26 |
| Margin after servicing, before loss | 3,438.36 | 3,438.36 |
| Hypothetical loss assumption | 1% annual EL, prorated 45/365 | 2% PD for this loan × 40% LGD |
| Expected loss | 739.73 | 4,800.00 |
| Net after expected loss | **2,698.63** | **−1,361.64** |

A 1% annual expected-loss rate is **not** 1% annual PD. At 40% LGD its prorated loss corresponds arithmetically to about 0.3082% PD over this 45-day loan; that is a translation of assumptions, not observed credit performance. Break-even per-loan PD under the common inputs is **1.4326% at 40% LGD**. Fixed overhead, liquidity reserves, tax and extraordinary workouts are excluded. Neither case deserves “realistic forecast” status without borrower and recovery data.

**Added in review: the borrower-rate sensitivity.** Claude's original base used a 12% borrower rate. At 12%, with the same principal, tenor, 10% capital and USD1,000 servicing, margin before loss is USD479.45. Break-even per-loan PD at 40% LGD is then 0.1998%. At bank-like borrower rates, almost any credible loss assumption removes the spread. The only external loss anchor in either source set is ICC's finding of trade-finance default rates below 0.3% for bank-originated flows, which does not represent small independent chrome traders ([ICC Trade Register 2025](https://academy.iccwbo.org/international-trade/article/icc-trade-register-2025/)). **Added from the Claude original:** under Basel slotting, a commodity that "is not quoted… No appropriate hedging instruments" rates weak, and the research found no liquid chrome hedge. Lenders weigh legal control, liquidity and hedgeability, and better data does not change those. ([BCBS CRE33](https://www.bis.org/committees/bcbs/basel-framework/standard/cre/33/inforce/2019-12-15/published/2022-12-08))

Recovery adds another constraint. The copied model discounts price, missing quantity and sale value, then deducts recovery costs. Its severe scenario—35% price decline, 5% missing stock, a further 20% haircut and USD50,000 costs—leaves **USD444,000**, a USD156,000 principal loss before unpaid interest. This is a stress assumption, not a predicted incident. Seized ore is not withdrawable USDC; expected recovery proceeds cannot fund immediate pool withdrawals. Obtain real buyer routes, minimum parcels, recovery authority and elapsed-time evidence before funding.

**Added from the Claude original: chrome-specific recovery evidence.** Chrometco reported that exporting lower-grade ore "financed by and securitised to ABSA" had become uneconomical (2020–21). That is a named case of financed chrome stranded by logistics and prices ([JSE SENS](https://senspdf.jse.co.za/documents/2020/JSE/ISSE/CMO/Interim20.pdf)). Chinese buyers have renegotiated agreed SA chrome prices down by up to 13.7% (undated, [Fastmarkets](https://www.fastmarkets.com/?p=35432)), which supports the price-fall stresses above.

### Account economics matter more than export-share funnels

Claude’s export-share funnel is useful for asking how much output is captive, committed, eligible and reachable, but those percentages do not establish a market estimate. Use named qualifying accounts × actual eligible lots × price. The following reuses the Codex assumptions: USD1,500/lot, USD300 variable delivery cost and USD250,000 annual fixed cost. It models one charging route only.

| Hypothetical scenario | Accounts × lots/year | Revenue | Contribution before fixed cost | Operating result | Average credit capital if every lot financed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Discovery | 3 × 12 | USD54,000 | USD43,200 | −USD206,800 | USD2.66m |
| Working scenario | 10 × 24 | USD360,000 | USD288,000 | USD38,000 | USD17.75m |
| Expanded | 30 × 48 | USD2.16m | USD1.728m | USD1.478m | USD106.52m |

Break-even is **209 lots/year** under these assumptions. Halving working-scenario usage produces a USD106,000 loss; doubling variable cost produces a USD34,000 loss; increasing fixed cost to USD500,000 produces a USD212,000 loss. Enterprise licensing needs its own account/usage/price and procurement evidence; converting these lot fees into a licence is not extra revenue. Software revenue cannot supply the associated lending capital. **Added from the Claude original:** a smart-contract audit for a standard DeFi protocol is priced at about US$60–130k ([Zealynx, 2026](https://www.zealynx.io/blogs/audit-pricing-2026), vendor bands). This cost arises before any real funds are handled and is not in the USD250,000 fixed-cost scenario. A five-person SA team costs about US$0.26–0.33m a year (OfferZen salary data, R18/US$ assumed), consistent with that scenario. ## Physical control determines whether the record is financeable

### F. Five risks govern the investment decision

| Risk | Why it dominates | Required mitigation/test |
|---|---|---|
| Wrong payer or wrong constraint | Better records may not change credit or operating costs | Transaction reconstruction, actual rejection reasons, paid budget-owner commitment |
| False or unenforceable collateral | Perfect records can describe nonexistent goods or subordinate rights | Independent control, title/security review, physical-release drills |
| Costs exceed value | Verification, conversion and human review can absorb savings | Quoted incremental costs, matched baseline and paid renewal |
| Coordination or settlement failure | Signatures, providers or contracts can delay release and usable cash | Accepted authority, measured field time, approved money route and exception handling |
| Illiquid, concentrated recovery | Default, prices and corridor disruption can occur together | Conservative netbacks, exposure limits, buyer/workout drill and committed liquidity |

The fraud record explains the difference between document integrity and physical assurance. Claude’s notes cite a JSE silo audit reporting receipts for stock held elsewhere (full-fetch status not recorded), and fetched legal commentary on copied Access World receipts. These are warnings about receipt-to-stock correspondence and document authentication, not measured failure rates for chrome. The same notes report the Trafigura/Gupta substitution case, with the judgment linked but incomplete retrieval provenance; this report therefore does not rely on its loss amount or judicial wording. No such case proves Thetis would prevent the loss. ([JSE audit notice](https://clientportal.jse.co.za/Content/JSENoticesandCircularsItems/JSE%20Market%20Notice%2066620B%20CDM%20%20%20Independent%20Audit%20on%20Physical%20Deliveries%20of%20WMAZ%20at%20the%20end%20of%202019%2020%20Marketing%20Season.pdf); [WFW, 22 September 2023](https://www.wfw.com/articles/same-paper-in-same-paper-out/))

An internal pledge lock stops those receipt units being reused inside the protocol. It does not discover prior external security or the same ore reissued under another identity. Two signatures establish agreement, not honesty. Specify independent stockpile surveys, calibrated weights, dry/wet-tonne treatment, sampling, moisture, grade, sealed samples and an umpire for disputes. Reconcile opening stock plus additions minus authorised releases and justified losses against measured closing stock. Do not clone a parent assay after blending or splitting without accepted rules. SGS advertises relevant inspection; this is capability evidence, not an assurance contract with Thetis. Claude’s draft-survey tolerance snippets are not adopted as universal thresholds. ([SGS inspection](https://www.sgs.com/en/services/quality-and-quantity-mineral-and-metal-commodities-inspection))

**Added from the Claude original: illicit supply.** Analysts estimate that up to about 10% of SA chrome output moves through illicit channels ([Mining Indaba, 8 October 2025](https://miningindaba.com/articles/export-curbs-loom-as-south-africa-targets-ill); [GI-TOC](https://riskbulletins.globalinitiative.net/esa-obs-025/03-emergence-of-bulk-commodity-illegal-industry-in-south-african-chrome-mining.html)). These are estimates, not measured data. They are why origin evidence belongs at intake, and why informal stock with unresolved provenance stays outside the initial credit hypothesis.

Terminal commingling must be investigated, not assumed. Ask whether stock is segregated, fungible within specified grades or blended, how ownership balances are recorded and who authorises withdrawal. Similarly, a customs checkpoint is not automatically a custodian authorised to sign. Establish custody and legal authority at every step. The design’s demo redeems at port, while its broader narrative reaches the buyer: **is the financed endpoint port arrival/loading or buyer delivery, and what replaces the receipt’s control afterward?** Do not mandate retirement at loading without that answer. Carrier delivery documents and the relevant contracts must bridge any continuing exposure. ([SA Sea Transport Documents Act](https://www.saflii.org/za/legis/num_act/stda2000280.pdf))

Legal feasibility is structure-specific. South African movable-security legislation and Mozambique’s registry framework provide routes to examine, not proof that this token establishes priority. Counsel should map title, security perfection, creditor priority, insolvency, split bulk stock and cross-border continuity. Mozambique sources were only partly accessible in the prior research; this report makes no local enforceability conclusion. ([SA Act 57 of 1993](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/act57of1993.pdf); [Mozambique Law 19/2018](https://crgm.gov.mz/docs/EN_Lei%2019_2018.pdf))

**Current chrome export-permit status remains unverified.** Official Notice 6712 of 3 October 2025 invites comments; the February 2026 extension also concerns consultation. Neither proves commencement, and neither proves no later instrument exists. Obtain an operative instrument and applicable requirements from ITAC/counsel before asserting a mandatory permit. Label a demo permit hypothetical. ([Official notice](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/202510/53477gon6712.pdf); [ITAC notices](https://itac.org.za/export-control/government-gazette-notices/))

**Added from the Claude original:** ITAC's own October 2025 announcement said exporters "will now be required to secure an export permit prior to dispatching any chrome ore", although the gazette only invited comment ([ITAC](https://itac.org.za/government-initiates-control-measures-for-chrome-ore-exports-to-revitalise-local-industry/)). SMM reported on 13 October 2026 that the proposed permit system and export tax "remain unimplemented" and are "neither gazetted or enacted" ([SMM, 13 October 2026](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/104155251-smm-chromium-analysis-south-africas-chrome-ore-exports-drop-108-in-august-chinas-share-reaches-70)). That report is later than the February 2026 extension, but it is trade press, not the Gazette. The "unverified" status above therefore stands until an operative instrument is checked.

Map lender funding, USDC purchase, disbursement, ZAR cash-out, buyer payment, repayment and recovery separately. Record entities, residency, account/wallet control, providers and settlement terms. The design does not establish an offshore pool merely by using USDC. The May 2026 SARB/FSCA communication distinguishes crypto payment activities and financial-service authorisation from payment-system authorisation; the August statement concerns drafts. FIC obligations also depend on activities performed. Counsel and an Authorised Dealer must assess exchange control and the exact lending/payment/pool structure. Avoid both blanket illegality and blanket approval. **Added from the Claude original:** the specific primary rule is in the SARB Currency and Exchanges Manual for Authorised Dealers, section B.18(B). Exporters must receive the full foreign-currency proceeds within six months of shipment and "offer for sale to an Authorised Dealer the full foreign currency proceeds within 30 days after becoming entitled thereto", or deal with them under the CFC-account rules. SARB monitors receipt electronically against SARS customs declarations. Any design that pays buyer proceeds into a USDC pool must fit this rule; counsel and an Authorised Dealer decide how. ([AD Manual, 2026 version](https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/what-we-do/financial-surveillance/financial-surveillance-documents/2026/Currency%20and%20Exchanges%20Manual%20for%20Authorised%20Dealers.pdf)) ([SARB/FSCA](https://www.resbank.co.za/content/dam/sarb/what-we-do/payments-and-settlements/regulation-oversight-and-supervision/designation-notices/280526%20Joint%20Communication_Crypto%20assets%20for%20domestic%20payment%20purposes.pdf); [SARB drafts](https://www.resbank.co.za/en/home/publications/publication-detail-pages/media-releases/2026/crypto-assets); [FIC](https://www.fic.gov.za/accountable-institut/crypto-asset-service-providers/))

Insurance is a contract to inspect, not a box to tick. ## Conclusion

The next scarce resource is a documented decision from a buyer and a lender, not another broad market narrative. A network that accepts the evidence but refuses its price reveals a different problem from one that pays for automation but rejects token settlement. Keeping these outcomes separate lets Thetis discover a viable product without using hypothetical credit margins to justify software demand—or a polished prototype to justify lending risk.

### R5. The report reads the original design; the revised design differs on two points it raises

The combined report interprets behaviour from `Thetis_Project_Document.md` only. The separate `Thetis_Revised_Project_Document.md` (17 November 2026) changes two things that bear on questions raised above:

- **Port endpoint.** The report asks whether finance ends at port or at buyer delivery, and says "do not mandate retirement at loading". The revised design does retire the lot at loading. `record_loading` records the bill-of-lading reference and hash, and the lender's security is expected to continue under the bill of lading. `redeem` is limited to stockyard collections that are never shipped, with holder and custodian signing together. The design question is answered in the revised design. The commercial and legal question is not: whether a lender accepts that hand-off, and on what bill-of-lading terms, still needs lender and counsel confirmation.
- **When partial release can happen.** The report describes partial sale "before dispatch". In the revised design, partial release, transfer and split are allowed whenever the lot is `Stored`. That covers the mine stockpile and the port stockyard after arrival. They are refused while `InTransit` or committed to a shipment. The revised demo releases 2,000 t at the port terminal, not at the mine.

Neither difference contradicts the report's caution that "trade pieces while trucks move" would misdescribe the product.
